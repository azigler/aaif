## Why it works (the moves to reuse)

- **A meme is the delivery vehicle.** A recognizable pop-culture still + the AAIF
  logo does the hook; the copy plays off the image. (Same shape as the Kirby
  -inhaling-food GIF for the MCP-stateless post.) The meme makes it shareable;
  the copy makes it land.
- **The dialogue/setup-payoff format** ("THEM: …— / ME: …? yea, i am 💅💄") — a
  playful flex framing borrowed from the meme.
- **Glam-irreverent emoji register** — 💅💄🙌💖. Femme, fun, confident. Distinct
  from generic 🚀💡✨ corporate emojis.
- **Lowercase, slangy, warm.** "they got me gal," "dev darlings," "faves~," "ty …
  for the select." Trailing `~` for sing-song energy.
- **Warm network tag as a PS.** Tagging Rizel Scarlett with a real reason ("more
  excuses for livestreams 💖") — networking artifact + genuine warmth, not a
  transactional credit.
- **Confident ownership, zero apology.** No "humbled to announce," no "obligatory
  plug." Just the flex, played for fun.

## The rule for AAIF posts

**Quirky wrapper, real payload.** The silliness (meme + glam emojis + lowercase
flex) is the vehicle; underneath it there should be something genuinely useful —
a teaching hook, a real insight, a promise to do the homework for the audience.
The acceptance post's payload was "here's what I'll be working on + a warm tag."
A spec post's payload is "here's what actually changes and whether you need to
care." Never silliness with nothing underneath; never a dry teach with no fun.

Calibration knobs: meme/GIF as hook · glam-irreverent emoji set · lowercase +
slang ("u," "ur," "w/," "da comments," "y'all") · warm tags w/ a real reason ·
one ALL-CAPS word or two for emphasis · close on a peer question. Keep the AAIF
framing community-warm and vendor-neutral (the LinearB tie stays a closing link).

## Exemplar 3 — the launch post he rewrote from an agent draft (2026-10-01)

The post for the personal-site essay "From Muse to Genius to Factory"
(https://www.andrewzigler.com/feed/from-muse-to-genius-to-factory/), posted at
https://lnkd.in/p/g6fxARS6. aaif drafted 146 words; he hand-edited it to about 290 and
posted that. This is the most direct signal we have for a launch post: his text, diffed
against ours.

**What he posted (verbatim):**

> Most weeks it feels I'm searching for a lost city full of treasure, in the thickest jungle on the planet, covered in mosquito bites - "but I KNOW it must be out here, somewhere!"  🗺️
>
> Right now I have a spot marked on my map: "SOFTWARE FACTORY"
>
> chat, we're all hot on software factories rn. I even gave a LeadDev talk about this recently. But where are we _really_ trying to reach, when we set out into the jungle looking for "the factory"?
>
> "What do you mean, look for the factory? I'm going to build it."
>
> Oh no... I'm sorry to inform you of this. But each factory has to be FOUND, actually.
>
> Steve Yegge put this so clearly: "I didn't design Wheelhouse, just like I didn't design Gas Town. I excavated both of them."
>
> I've been chewing on how inspiration keeps changing address, and it plays an interesting role in that excavation. Back in my days studying Classics in college, I learned how Greeks called down a Muse, but the Romans HAD a genius, and somewhere around the Enlightenment we decided people ARE the genius, instead.
>
> Humanity's relationship with creation has taken many forms, and in the modern world, we only think about BEING the genius now. But AI shows up and shatters this norm.
>
> Now, when someone runs a fleet of agents, we say they HAVE a factory, which is the same grammar the Romans used, when talking about their genius. Are we going back in time? Maybe... 😅
>
> Flipping your creative instincts from building to discovery can feel like going upstream, so here are the primitives I've discovered for excavation your own muse, genius, factory, or whatever you want to call it:
>
> https://lnkd.in/gwK4wcpp

**The diff against our draft:**
- KEPT, nearly word for word: "I've been chewing on how inspiration keeps changing address"; the Muse / HAD / ARE arc; "when someone runs a fleet of agents, we say they HAVE a factory, which is the same grammar the Romans used"; 😅.
- CUT: our hook, a clever detail (the Muses' "hallucination disclaimer", the Hesiod quote, the "inspire" etymology). He also folded our closing peer question ("What are y'all calling the thing you're building?") into the CTA ("or whatever you want to call it"), and dropped 📜 and 🐸.
- ADDED: a lost-city expedition scene as the opener, with his inner voice quoted; "chat," and "rn"; a cross-reference to his own LeadDev talk; a staged objection and reply ("I'm going to build it." / "Oh no... each factory has to be FOUND, actually."); the Yegge excavation quote; his personal backstory ("Back in my days studying Classics in college"); a stakes line ("AI shows up and shatters this norm"); a playful question after the grammar point ("Are we going back in time? Maybe..."); a CTA framed as the reader's own shift ("Flipping your creative instincts from building to discovery...").

**Lessons for the next launch-post draft:**
1. **The ONE insight is the article's thesis, not its cleverest detail.** We applied the go-deep-on-one-insight rule to a fun factoid. He went deep on the core turn (factories are found, not built) and dramatized it. Pick the idea the article exists to make.
2. **Open on a scene that IS the metaphor.** The jungle expedition carries the excavation thesis before it is ever stated. A "fun fact" opener is a hook; a scene is the argument in costume.
3. **Stage the objection as dialogue, then correct it playfully.** The quoted "I'm going to build it." plus "Oh no... I'm sorry to inform you" is his teaching move: voice the reader's assumption, then flip it warmly.
4. **"Short and sweet" means ~250-300 words to him, not ~150.** He expanded our draft by about 2x. This matches the 2026-09-04 teaser finding: he writes longer and warmer than agents assume.
5. **Cross-reference his own recent work** (the LeadDev talk) and credit the thinker whose words he builds on (Yegge), in the post itself.
6. **The CTA names the reader's transformation** ("from building to discovery") and absorbs the peer question.
7. **His register is louder than the skill's defaults.** He uses ALL CAPS on 7 words (KNOW, SOFTWARE FACTORY, FOUND, HAD, ARE, BEING, HAVE), streamer slang ("chat,"), an underscore-italic (_really_), and a spaced hyphen as a dash. Don't "correct" these in his own text. Its typos ("excavation your own") are his to keep or fix.
